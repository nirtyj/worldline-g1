### 1.1 Launch

- Headless always; `--enable_cameras` is added automatically when `--camera` is not `none` or `--tp-camera`.
- `--rt-pace` is the default (`--no-rt-pace` free-runs, for measurements only). `--physx-device cpu` is the default
  and the measured choice (§1.10).
- Readiness: P1 prints one line `WL_ISAAC_READY {json}` (ports, house, spawn, floor_z, physx_device, ...) once
  lowstate is flowing and the band is **on**, then answers `ping`. The ZMQ server sockets are bound first, so a
  second instance on the same ports fails in about 1 s with `Address already in use`.
- The G1 USD is built once: `python -m sim_isaac.g1_asset --build` (writes `/work/worldline-g1/assets/g1/`).

### 1.2 Robot model (training parity)

- URDF: SONIC's training `gear_sonic/data/assets/robot_description/urdf/g1/main.urdf` with the 14 Dex3 hand joints
  switched from `fixed` to `revolute` (axis/limit/origin copied from
  `gear_sonic/data/robot_model/model_data/g1/g1_29dof_with_hand.urdf`; they are identical, checked), mesh paths made
  absolute, converted with Isaac Lab `UrdfConverter` (`fix_base=False`, `merge_fixed_joints=True`,
  `replace_cylinders_with_capsules=True`, drive gains 0), exactly the options of `robots/g1.py:199-222`.
  Result: 43 joints, 44 bodies.
- Actuators: the `actuators` dict of `G1_CYLINDER_MODEL_12_DEX_CFG` (`gear_sonic/envs/manager_env/robots/g1.py:238-357`),
  **imported** from gear_sonic, not copied: implicit PD, armature, `effort_limit_sim`, `velocity_limit_sim`. Dex3
  joints: an extra implicit group (armature 0.01 like the MuJoCo `finger_motor` class, `g1_29dof_with_hand.xml:20-22`;
  effort/velocity limits from the URDF; initial kp/kd 1.5/0.1 = the deploy's Dex3 hold, `dex3_hands.hpp:308-332`).
- Articulation root: self-collisions on, solver 8 position / 4 velocity iterations (`g1.py:214-218`); rigid props and
  `activate_contact_sensors=True` as in training.
- Physics: dt = 0.005 s (`base_env.yaml:32`; MuJoCo `SIMULATE_DT`), TGS, ground/house floor friction static =
  dynamic = 1.0 combine `multiply` (`modular_tracking_env_cfg.py:319-330`), `gpu_max_rigid_patch_count` as training.
- PD (`--pd implicit`, default): the lowcmd `kp`/`kd` are written into the PhysX drives (only when they change by
  more than 1%), `q`/`dq` become drive targets, `tau` is a feed-forward actuation force. The deploy's `kps`/`kds`
  equal the training stiffness/damping (`policy_parameters.hpp:143-208` vs `g1.py:15-26`): training parity.
  `--pd explicit`: `clip(tau + kp(q*-q) + kd(dq*-dq), ±effort_limit)` at 200 Hz like MuJoCo `base_sim.py:258-288,423`.
- Before the first `rt/lowcmd` the joints hold the default standing pose with the training gains (MuJoCo applies
  zero torque; deliberate deviation so the robot does not flop on the band). After that the latest lowcmd is applied
  every physics step and never timed out (MuJoCo semantics).
- A static PD stand with the training gains **cannot** balance (measured: falls 1.7 s after band release; ankle kp
  28.5 Nm/rad vs a toppling stiffness m·g·h ≈ 240 Nm/rad). Balance is SONIC's job; release the band only after the
  deploy is in CONTROL.

### 1.3 DDS (unitree_sdk2py + cyclonedds 0.10.2, `unitree_hg` IDL, same types as the real G1 and the MuJoCo bridge)

P1 mirrors `gear_sonic/utils/mujoco_sim/unitree_sdk2py_bridge.py` + `base_sim.py`:

P1 is **controller-agnostic by construction**: it exposes the real G1's low-level Unitree DDS interface (`rt/lowcmd`
in; `rt/lowstate` + `rt/secondary_imu` + Dex3 out) and nothing SONIC-specific. Any controller that drives a real G1
through unitree_sdk2 low-level mode can drive P1 unchanged; e.g. the Decoupled WBC (GR00T N1.5/N1.6) in the same
repo publishes `rt/lowcmd` with the same SDK (`decoupled_wbc/control/envs/g1/utils/command_sender.py:45`). Swapping
the controller is a P2 (+ P3 command adapter) change only; P1 is untouched.

| Topic | Dir | Type | Content |
|---|---|---|---|
| `rt/lowstate` | PUB, every physics step (200 Hz) | `LowState_` | `motor_state[0..28].{q,dq,ddq,tau_est}` in Unitree motor order; `imu_state.quaternion` = pelvis orientation **wxyz** in world; `gyroscope` = pelvis angular velocity in the **pelvis frame**; `accelerometer` = pelvis linear acceleration in the **world frame without gravity** (MuJoCo `qacc[:3]`, bridge `:185-194`); `tick = int(t_sim·1000)` (`:202`); `mode_machine` = `--mode-machine` (default 0: the MuJoCo bridge never sets it); `crc` = the SDK CRC (`--crc`, default on; the deploy in sim runs `--disable-crc-check` anyway, `deploy.sh:405`). `tau_est` = clipped PD torque estimate |
| `rt/secondary_imu` | PUB, 200 Hz | `IMUState_` | `quaternion` = torso_link orientation wxyz in world; `gyroscope` = torso_link angular velocity in the **torso frame** (bridge `:196-197`, `base_sim.py:359-371`). The deploy refuses to run without it |
| `rt/odostate` | PUB, 200 Hz | `OdoState_` | `position`, `linear_velocity` (world, pelvis origin), `orientation` (**wxyz**, as the MuJoCo bridge writes it despite the IDL comment), `angular_velocity` (pelvis frame) |
| `rt/dex3/{left,right}/state` | PUB, 200 Hz | `HandState_` | `motor_state[0..6].{q,dq}` in Dex3 order |
| `rt/lowcmd` | SUB | `LowCmd_` | `motor_cmd[0..28].{q,dq,kp,kd,tau}`; the newest sample is taken every physics step |
| `rt/dex3/{left,right}/cmd` | SUB | `HandCmd_` | `motor_cmd[0..6].{q,dq,kp,kd,tau}`; newest sample every 4th physics step (50 Hz) |

Motor order (hardware = MuJoCo order, `robot_parameters.hpp:90-130`, `g1_29dof_sonic_model12.yaml:119-148`):
legs 0-11 (`left_hip_pitch, left_hip_roll, left_hip_yaw, left_knee, left_ankle_pitch, left_ankle_roll`, then right),
waist 12-14 (`yaw, roll, pitch`), left arm 15-21 (`shoulder_pitch, shoulder_roll, shoulder_yaw, elbow, wrist_roll,
wrist_pitch, wrist_yaw`), right arm 22-28. Dex3 order per hand (MJCF order used by the MuJoCo bridge,
`base_sim.py:228-240`): `thumb_0, thumb_1, thumb_2, middle_0, middle_1, index_0, index_1`. Isaac joints are mapped
**by name** (joint-map test: every motor moves the named Isaac joint, §1.9).

Implementation (mechanics only, content unchanged): a fixed-layout CDR codec (`sim_isaac/fastdds.py`) replaces
cyclonedds-python's pure-Python (de)serializer (~3 ms/step). Every codec is checked at start-up against the library
byte-for-byte (writers, incl. the LowState CRC over the C struct layout) or value-for-value (readers); the check
result is logged as `codec_verified={...}` and a failed check falls back to the library path. Readers use the SDK's
default QoS (KEEP_LAST 1) and are polled once per physics step instead of a listener thread per sample (the MuJoCo
bridge also only uses the newest command, `queueLen=1`).

Lowstate heartbeat: if physics publishes no lowstate for `--heartbeat-ms` (default 50 ms, e.g. a render hitch), a
thread re-publishes the last `rt/lowstate` + `rt/secondary_imu` so the deploy's 500 ms `LOW_STATE_ABSENT_THRESHOLD`
(`g1_deploy_onnx_ref.cpp:299,2797-2812`) never fires on a hitch. Counted in `get_stats.heartbeat_pubs`.

The cyclonedds 0.10.2 C library on the box is built with `-D_FORTIFY_SOURCE=0` (`/work/opt/cyclonedds-0.10.2`):
with Ubuntu 24.04's default fortify level, creating a domain with a network interface aborts with
`*** buffer overflow detected ***`. Anyone running unitree_sdk2py in `/work/envs/isaaclab` gets this build.

### 1.4 Camera (PUB 5565, gear_sonic sensor_server format)

- Mount: a child prim of `torso_link` at the `d435_link` offset `xyz=(0.0576235, 0.01753, 0.41987)`,
  `rpy=(0, 0.8307767, 0)` (`g1_29dof_with_hand.urdf:614-619`; `main.urdf` has no `d435_link`). It looks ~48° down,
  so it mostly sees the floor and the hands in front of the robot, like the real head camera.
- 640x480 pinhole, vertical FOV 45° (MuJoCo `head_camera` default `fovy`), square pixels (HFOV 57.8°), clipping
  0.05-50 m, rendered every 1/30 s of **sim time** (`IMAGE_DT = 0.0333`, `base_sim.py:70`).
- Wire format, byte-compatible with `gear_sonic/utils/mujoco_sim/image_publish_utils.py:165-185` +
  `sensor_server.py:57-77`: one ZMQ frame = `msgpack.packb(d, use_bin_type=True)` with
  `d = {"timestamps": {"ego_view": <wall time.time()>}, "images": {"ego_view": <b64 str>}, "ego_view": <b64 str>,
  "t_sim": float, "seq": int}`, `<b64 str>` = `base64(cv2.imencode(".jpg", rgb, [IMWRITE_JPEG_QUALITY, 80]))`.
  As in MuJoCo, the RGB array goes to `cv2.imencode` unchanged, so `ImageUtils.decode_image` returns **RGB**.
  `t_sim`/`seq` are extras that gear_sonic consumers ignore. Encoding runs on a worker thread; SNDHWM 20.

### 1.5 PUB 5601 (ground truth stream)

Multipart `[topic, msgpack(payload)]`. Subscribe with `setsockopt(zmq.SUBSCRIBE, b"gt.pose")` (no CONFLATE).

`gt.pose` at 50 Hz of sim time (every 4th physics step):

```
{ "seq": int, "t_sim": float, "t_wall": float, "rtf": float|null,   # rtf over the last ~1 s
  "base_pos": [x,y,z],            # pelvis origin, world, m
  "base_quat_wxyz": [w,x,y,z],    # pelvis orientation, world
  "base_lin_vel_w": [vx,vy,vz],   # pelvis origin velocity, world frame
  "base_ang_vel_w": [wx,wy,wz],   # world frame
  "yaw": float,                   # rad, CCW from world +x, in (-pi, pi]
  "pelvis_z": float,              # base_pos[2] - floor_z (standing: ~0.75)
  "fallen": bool,                 # pelvis_z < 0.45 m or pelvis up-axis tilt > 60 deg
  "foot_contact": {"left": bool, "right": bool},   # ankle_roll_link net contact force > 20 N
  "band": bool,                   # elastic band engaged
  "lowcmd_age_s": float|null      # seconds since the last fresh rt/lowcmd (null = never)
}
```

`gt.event` (sporadic): `{"t_sim", "t_wall", "event": "band"|"reset_robot"|"fallen"|"recovered", ...}`.

### 1.7 Elastic band

Port of `ElasticBand` (`unitree_sdk2py_bridge.py:366-395`): a world-frame wrench on the pelvis every physics step,
force `kp_pos·(anchor − pos) − kd_pos·vel` (`kp_pos = 10000, kd_pos = 1000`), torque `−kp_ang·rotvec − kd_ang·ω`
(`kp_ang = 1000, kd_ang = 10`). Differences from MuJoCo: the anchor is `(x, y)` of the pelvis at engage time and
`floor_z + z` (default 0.80 m: feet just on the floor, pelvis ~0.77 m), and the orientation target is upright with
the **yaw at engage time** (MuJoCo drives to yaw 0). `ramp_s > 0` scales the gains to zero linearly in sim time.

### 1.8 Scene plugin (house agent) and extra frames

`--house <id>` calls `scenes/loader.py: load_house(stage, house_id[, dynamic_objects=...]) -> HouseInfo` after the
physics scene exists and before the robot spawns (no InteractiveScene). P1 reads `house_id`, `floor_z`, `spawn`,
`bounds`, `rooms`, `objects`, `occupancy_npz`, `to_scene_info()`; the spawn defaults to `HouseInfo.spawn`
(`--spawn x,y,yaw` overrides). Default lights (dome + distant) are added. `--house empty`: 30 m × 30 m ground plane.

`--tp-camera` (optional, costs a second render product per render, ~+7 ms/render): a third-person chase camera on
PUB 5602, multipart `[b"frame.tp", msgpack{seq, t_sim, t_wall, jpeg (bytes; decodes to RGB), base_pos, yaw}]` at
`--tp-hz` (10). `sim_isaac/tools/record_video.py --ego --tp --topdown` writes `ego.mp4`, `tp.mp4` and a zero-cost
`topdown.mp4` (gt.pose drawn over the cached top-down render).

Per-step cost (house + camera, paced): lowcmd pull + target write 0.25 ms, band 0.14 ms, **PhysX step 1.1 ms**,
state read 0.43 ms, DDS publish (5 topics) 0.17 ms; render 8.6 ms every 1/30 s of sim time. Per sim second:
200 × 2.08 + 30 × 8.6 ≈ 0.67 s of wall time, i.e. ~30% headroom (the free-running run confirms 1.29).

**Decision: the primary path.** P1 runs the Isaac-side Unitree DDS bridge on **CPU PhysX at 200 Hz with wall-clock
pacing**, and the **unmodified** C++ `gear_sonic_deploy` (zmq_manager, planner topic) drives it on its own wall
clock, exactly as it drives the MuJoCo sim and the real robot. Reasons:
1. Measured RTF with 1 G1 + house + head camera at 30 Hz is 0.998 total and 1.000 over 10 s windows, with ~30%
   free-running headroom; with the deploy in the loop 0.993 / 1.000.
2. The unmodified deploy stands (60 s), walks (2.5 m), stops and turns the G1 in the MolmoSpaces house through
   P1 with joint-level control only (§1.9); no root writes after spawn.
3. GPU PhysX is rejected: for one robot its pipeline overhead makes the PhysX step 4× (bare) to 17× (house) slower
   than CPU PhysX (RTF 0.95 bare, 0.22 in the house).
4. The SONIC fallbacks (a) in-process lockstep SONIC and (b) a sim-clock deploy patch are therefore **not needed for
   M1**. They stay the documented contingency if RTF drops (e.g. several cameras, a heavier house, a busy box).

Residual risks (known issues):
- **Render hitches.** `sim.render()` occasionally takes 70-190 ms (a few per minute, cause inside Kit/RTX; not
  Python GC: `gc.freeze()` after start-up leaves only gen-0 collections of ≤ 0.1 ms in the loop). The pacer drops
  lag beyond 100 ms (`overruns`, `lost_s` in `get_stats`: 0.1-0.5 s per minute); the lowstate heartbeat thread keeps
  `rt/lowstate` flowing (`heartbeat_pubs`: 2-9 per run), so the deploy's 500 ms `LOW_STATE_ABSENT_THRESHOLD`
  never fires. SONIC walked through them in every run.
- **CPU contention.** P1 uses ~2.8 cores (`process_cpu_pct` 280%) and the deploy is wall-clock sensitive: other jobs
  on the box lower the worst 1 s RTF (0.88 → 0.77) and made the deploy agent's MuJoCo reference fall. Run the
  integrated stack on a quiet box; `ISAAC_TASKSET` / `DEPLOY_TASKSET` pinning is available in `m1_up.sh`.
- The contract's `rtf` in `gt.pose` (1 s window) and `get_stats` (`rtf_*`, `overruns`, `lost_s`, `hitches_gt25ms`,
  `gc`) let P3 and the drive test record the RTF actually achieved in every run (E5).

### 3.2 SONIC input (P3 → P2, PUB 5556): SonicMux

- Wire: `[topic][1280-byte null-padded JSON header][LE payload]`, one frame. `body/wire.py` is a verbatim port of
  `gear_sonic/utils/teleop/zmq/zmq_planner_sender.py:14-158`; `body/tests/test_wire.py` asserts **byte equality**
  with the upstream module. Receiver rules: `zmq_packed_message_subscriber.hpp:99, 272-329`, `zmq_manager.hpp:668-800`.
- `command`: `{start:u8, stop:u8, planner:u8}`. The body sends `start=1, stop=0, planner=1` (3×) on `stand` only.
  `stop=1` is sent **only** by the `shutdown_control` op (m1_down.sh, band on first): it makes the deploy run
  `Stop()` → `CreateDampingCommand()` (kp=0, kd=8) and exit (`g1_deploy_onnx_ref.cpp:2718-2745, 4513-4530`).
  A user `stop` is planner IDLE, never `command{stop}`.
- `planner`: `mode:i32, movement:f32[3], facing:f32[3], speed:f32, height:f32(-1)`, re-sent at **50 Hz** (≥10 Hz
  needed: 1 s timeout to IDLE, `zmq_manager.hpp:581-642`) from bind until exit, IDLE `movement=[0,0,0]` when no
  motion runs. Directions within 2° and speeds within 0.03 m/s of the last sent message reuse the exact previous
  f32 values, so noise does not force deploy re-plans (`g1_deploy_onnx_ref.cpp:3659-3732`).
- Modes used: 0 IDLE (stand, stop, turn in place with a new `facing`, as keyboard Q/E do,
  `keyboard_handler.hpp:556-566`), 1 SLOW_WALK with `speed` clamped to 0.2–0.8 m/s (`keyboard_handler.hpp:267-272`),
  ≤0.4 m/s when the motion is mostly sideways (`docs/source/tutorials/keyboard.md` strafe tip). WALK/RUN unused.
- **Planner frame.** `movement`/`facing` are in the planner frame whose +x is the pelvis heading when the planner
  was initialised (`localmotion_kplanner.hpp:591-624`; policy mapping `g1_deploy_onnx_ref.cpp:560-606`). The body
  converts world directions with `R(-(θ0 + δ - b))`, where `θ0 = yaw(init_base_quat)`, `δ = delta_heading`, both read
  from `g1_debug` (`zmq_output_handler.hpp:43-45`); until the first `g1_debug` arrives, `θ0` = GT yaw captured when
  `start` is sent. `b` is a slow integral of the GT heading error, learned only while IDLE, settled and with an
  unchanged facing for > 1.5 s (clamped to ±20°).
- Watchdog in the mux: if the 50 Hz control loop has not refreshed a non-IDLE command for 0.3 s, the mux sends IDLE
  with the last facing instead of repeating the stale walk.

### 3.4 Control API: ROUTER 5610 / PUB 5611

Request (DEALER or REQ, one JSON frame): `{"id": str (optional, echoed), "op": str, "args": {...}}`, optionally with

go_to with the `nav2` backend waits at most ~50 ms per request (bridge ping + goal check + plan + accept; the goto

Rules: one motion at a time; a new motion (or `stop`) cancels the active one, which ends `canceled` with
`data.reason = "preempted"` (+ `by`), `"stop"`, `"halt"` (+ `halt_epoch`, `halt_reason`; §3.10), `"superseded"` or
`"released"` (§3.11). The fences (§3.11) gate `stand, walk, turn_to, go_to, velocity, approach, arm, arm_script,
scan` (`halted`, `stale_command`, `body_busy`); `stop`, `halt`, `resume`, `recover`, `status`, `ping`, `hello`, `bye`,
`release`, `clear_fault`, `reload_map` and `shutdown_control` are never gated. Motion ops other than `stand`/`stop` need the deploy in CONTROL
(`not_standing` otherwise). Failure reasons: `not_standing, no_pose, pose_stale, deploy_stale, deploy_not_running,
deploy_not_ready, deploy_not_in_control, band_release_failed, pelvis_out_of_band, fallen, timeout, no_path,
goal_in_obstacle, start_in_obstacle, stuck, off_path, final_error, no_occupancy, bad_args, fault:fallen,
fault:deploy_lost, halted, stale_command, body_busy`; `approach` also `too_far, path_blocked` (and `goal_in_obstacle`,
`final_error`); `recover` also `no_fault, band_failed, reset_failed, band_release_failed, recovery_unstable,
recovery_fell` (and `deploy_not_running`: path B, a P2 restart); go_to with
the `nav2` backend also: `nav2_unavailable` (bridge unreachable at start, no goto reply within 8 s, or no status
reply for 3 s mid-goal), `nav2_rejected` (Nav2 refused the goal), `nav2_canceled` (the Nav2 goal was canceled by
someone else), `nav2_failed` / `nav2_aborted` (Nav2 failed without a mapped error code), `nav2_config` (invalid
planner / controller), `tf_error`; `velocity` also `stale_command` (first message older than `watchdog_s`). Both go_to
backends give the same reason for the same goal (the bridge replicates A*'s goal rule): a goal closer than 0.25 m to an
obstacle or unknown cell, or outside the map, is snapped to the nearest free cell within 0.5 m, else
`goal_in_obstacle`; a free goal that cannot be reached is `no_path`.

**`velocity` (streaming).** The first message starts the op (normal `accepted` event, pre-empts the active motion).
Every later message **must carry the same `stream`** (default: the op id, i.e. `data.stream` of the accepted reply):
it then only updates the twist, replies `state: "done"`, publishes **no event** and creates no op record. A message
without a matching `stream` starts a **new** op (and cancels the old one). `end: true` stops the stream. Messages
whose `t_wall` is older than `watchdog_s` are dropped. No fresh message for `watchdog_s` ⇒ planner IDLE (holding the
last commanded facing); the op ends `succeeded, ended_by: "watchdog"` once settled (a stream that resumes before
that continues the op). Conversion: `|v| < 0.05` ⇒ no translation, else SLOW_WALK in direction `R(yaw_gt)·(vx, vy)`
at clamp(|v|, 0.2, 0.8) (≤ 0.4 strafing); `wz` is integrated into a facing that SONIC gets in steps like `turn_to`:
≤ 30° from the last commanded facing, only while the body is within 15° of it, and in place (IDLE) at most one step
per 0.4 s. Messages with `args.goal_id` are Nav2's `/cmd_vel` from `nav2/ros_bridge.py`: they only feed the active
go_to with that id, never start an op; otherwise they are rejected with `error: "stale_goal"` (no event).
A fall (`gt.pose.fallen` or pelvis_z < 0.55 m after start) fails the active op, holds IDLE, latches
`fault="fallen"`, engages P1's band and publishes `body.fault{kind: "fell"}`; `recover` runs the soft recovery
(§3.12), or after a P1 `reset_robot` send `clear_fault` (and `stand` again if needed).

go_to backends: **`nav2` (default)**: ROS 2 Nav2 through `nav2/ros_bridge.py` (Smac 2D planner, Regulated Pure
Pursuit, humanoid behaviour tree; docs/nav2.md). Before each go_to the body pings the bridge (≤ 50 ms; "unreachable" is
cached 3 s) and falls back to `astar` when it does not answer or Nav2 is not active (`backend_fallback` says why). All
bridge calls are asynchronous, so the 50 Hz loop never waits on ROS. Result semantics under nav2: `replans` = goal
re-sends by the body (escape steps, retries, re-approaches; Nav2's own 1 Hz replanning is not counted),
`stuck_events` = Nav2 `stuck` failures (`FAILED_TO_MAKE_PROGRESS`, `PATIENCE_EXCEEDED`, `NO_VALID_CONTROL`) with the
body's reaction (`escape`, `escape+retry`, `retry`, `none`), `nav2_recoveries` = Nav2 BT recoveries. A stuck goal is
re-sent once (`max_nav_retries`), after a slow ≤ 0.6 m escape step when the clearance is below the inflation radius
(0.45 m); a start inside the inscribed zone (< 0.30 m) gets an escape step first (≤ 2 per go_to).

**`astar`** (fallback) algorithm: A* (8-connected, octile, clearance-weighted cost `1 + 2·exp(-(clearance - r)/0.25)`) on a 0.10 m
grid sampled from the 0.05 m inflated grid, greedy line-of-sight shortcutting on the fine grid, 0.10 m resampling;
pure pursuit (lookahead 0.6 m, facing = direction of travel, speed `min(v, sqrt(2·0.35·d)+0.05)` ≥ 0.2); turn in place
first when the heading error > 50–70°; arrival 0.15 m → IDLE settle → up to 2 slow holonomic corrections → optional
final turn → check. Stuck = commanded motion but < 0.08 m progress in 3 s → IDLE, virtual 0.25 m obstacle ahead,
re-plan (max 2); cross-track > 0.6 m → re-plan.

6. (v0.6) `deploy_lost` after 1.0 s without `g1_debug`, not PLAN §6.6's 300 ms (§3.12).
7. (v0.6) A lost runtime session is an internal halt (latched, `runtime_lost`) and only while the body moves; PLAN

8. (v0.6) `acquire` from a newer generation supersedes the lease; PLAN §5.5 rejects every acquire while a lease is

9. (v0.6) `body.state` stays at 5 Hz (PLAN §6.6: 20 Hz); every change that matters has its own topic (§3.10-§3.12).
10. (v0.6) `policy_lost` is an event, not a FAULT mode: the arm session's `hold_on_end` already holds the robot.

### 3.9 Arm channel: op `arm` (recommendation (b), owner decision 2026-09-29)

GR00T drives the G1's **arms and hands** with joint targets; SONIC stays the only body controller and keeps legs and
balance; walking still comes from the planner (mode/movement/facing/speed). Code: `body/arm.py` (ArmChannel),
`body/sonic_mux.py` (`set_upper` / `clear_upper`), `body/joint_map.py` (orders, limits, Dex3), `body/g1_kin.py`
(FK/IK for tests and scripted poses). Measured tracking: `docs/arm_tracking.md`.

**Wire.** While a stream is active, **every** planner message SonicMux sends (50 Hz, whatever the leg motion) also
carries `upper_body_position` f32[17], `upper_body_velocity` f32[17] and, once the client has sent hand targets,
both `left_hand_joints` and `right_hand_joints` f32[7] (`zmq_manager.hpp:885-980`). The 17 values are in SONIC's
IsaacLab-interleaved order (`policy_parameters.hpp:80-81`, `g1_deploy_onnx_ref.cpp:784-791`): waist yaw/roll/pitch,
then left/right pairs joint by joint; `joint_map.wire_from_mj17` is the only place that builds it (PLAN §6 invariant 8,
`body/tests/test_joint_map.py` pins it to both upstream sources). Dex3 order: thumb_0, thumb_1, thumb_2, middle_0,
middle_1, index_0, index_1 (`base_sim.py:225-240`); q = 0 is open. The deploy re-evaluates the override on every
consumed message and drops it with the first message without the fields (`zmq_manager.hpp:581-596`) or after 1 s
without any planner message (`:618-628`). The targets replace the upper-body joints of the reference motion the
encoder sees (all 10 future frames); SONIC's policy then tracks them (no direct PD path). Hands go straight to
`rt/dex3/*/cmd` (kp 1.5, kd 0.1, ≤ 0.25 rad from the measured position per write, `dex3_hands.hpp:114-190`).
Velocity: `zero` (default, = upstream, which never sends it) or `est` (d target/dt from the client's `t_wall`) or `cmd`
(client's `upper_body_vel`); always sent, so a stale velocity can never persist in the deploy's buffer. Both hands
are always sent together: the deploy reuses the last value it received for a hand missing from a message.

**Input.** `upper_body`: a dict `{joint_name: rad}` (partial: the other joints keep their previous target; the first
message of a session starts from SONIC's current reference) or a list of 17 in **named MuJoCo order**
(`UPPER_BODY_MUJOCO_JOINTS` = waist 3, left arm 7, right arm 7). Unknown names / wrong length ⇒ `bad_args`, with no
state change. Targets are clamped to the URDF limits − 0.02 rad (`stats.clamped`) and slew-limited to `max_vel`
(6 rad/s; `stats.slew_limited_ticks`). Waist: `waist: "ref"` (default for a dict without waist joints) sends SONIC's
own reference waist (`g1_debug.body_q_target`); `"cmd"` sends the client's (default when the dict names waist joints).
A message is applied to the mux immediately (≤ 20 ms to the wire), not at the next control tick.

**Servo (default on).** SONIC tracks the override with a pose-dependent steady-state error (0.1-0.3 rad raw) and
~150 ms of lag (`docs/arm_tracking.md`). The channel integrates the error of the 14 measured arm joints
(`g1_debug.body_q`) against the target `servo_delay_s` ago: `corr += servo_ki·dt·(target(t − 0.15 s) − q)`,
`|corr| ≤ servo_max`, sent = target + corr (clamped to the limits). Frozen while `g1_debug` is older than 0.1 s; reset
with each session. It does not remove the lag: a client that knows its future targets (a GR00T chunk) should send them
~0.15 s ahead. Measured effect: held-pose palm error 17-75 mm → 2-23 mm, carrying while walking 42-45 → 9-17 mm RMS.

**Watchdog and hand-back.** States `off → stream → hold → blend → off`. No message for `watchdog_s` (0.3 s) ⇒ `hold`:
the last pose is re-sent for `hold_s` (1.0 s); then `blend`: min-jerk over `blend_s` (1.5 s) from the held pose to
SONIC's **policy-default** arms, i.e. its own live reference (`g1_debug.body_q_target`, the planner motion without
the override; IDLE: elbows ≈ 1.02, shoulder yaw ≈ ∓0.61), hands to the deploy's default fist; then the override is
dropped (the last override equals the reference, so the release is seamless). A new message from the owner during
hold/blend resumes the stream. `end: true` ⇒ blend now (`ended_by: client`); `stop {arms: true}` ⇒ blend, op ends
`canceled` (reason `stop`). A fall (fault) or service shutdown drops the override at once. If wl-body dies, the
deploy's 1 s planner timeout drops it.

**Ownership (one owner at a time), by `stream`.** (1) With the channel off, any stream may start it: the first message
is an op (`accepted` / `progress` 1 Hz / `succeeded`|`canceled`|`failed`), later messages with the same `stream` are
updates (reply `done`, no events). Needs `in_control` and no fault (`not_standing`, `fault:fallen`). (2) While the
owner streams, another stream is rejected `arm_busy` unless it sends `preempt: true`: it then continues from the pose
being sent, the old op ends `canceled` (reason `preempted`) and the old stream gets `arm_preempted` while the new
owner streams (once the arms are free again it may start a new session). (3) Once the owner
has stopped streaming (hold or blend), any stream may take over without `preempt` (old op `canceled`, reason
`taken_over`). (4) The legs are never owned by this channel: motion ops (`walk`, `velocity`, `turn_to`, `go_to`,
`stop`) keep the legs and do not touch the arms (except `stop {arms: true}`), so arm streaming composes with IDLE
and with walking. `t_wall` older than `watchdog_s` ⇒ `stale_command` (dropped).

### 3.10 Halt lane: PULL 5612 (M2b B.1; owner body-core)

The runtime's halt (PLAN §5.6) must latch HOLD within 30 ms and must never wait behind the control loop, which can
be busy for tens of milliseconds (an A* plan at a go_to start, a P1 call). So a halt has its own socket and thread
(`body/halt.py`), and does only thread-safe work before it acknowledges.

**Wire.** PUSH → PULL 5612, one JSON frame: `{"op": "halt", "epoch": int, "t_wall": sender time.time(), "reason"?:
str}` or `{"op": "resume", "epoch": int}`. The same two exist as ROUTER ops (`halt`, `resume`, §3.4) for tools; the
lane is the fast path. Malformed lane messages are counted (`body.state.halt.lane.bad`) and never guessed.

**On a halt `{epoch: n}`**, in this order, on the lane thread:
1. SonicMux latch: every planner message becomes IDLE, movement 0, facing = the current GT yaw (or the last facing
   sent if gt.pose is stale), whatever the motions still set, and the sender thread is woken to send it at once
   (not at the next 20 ms keepalive). Never `command{stop}`.
2. `ArmChannel.latch(n, "halt")` (body-arm, §3.9): arms held at the measured pose, hands at their measured q, the
   active arm op ends `canceled` (reason halt); arm messages with `control_epoch <= n` are rejected `halted`.
3. The fence latch (§3.11): `latched = true`, `halt_epoch = n`.
4. PUB `[b"body.halted", {epoch, requested_epoch, kind: new|repeat|stale, reason, source: lane|op|watchdog, t_mono
   (body monotonic at publish), t_recv_mono, handle_ms, t_wall_sent, arms_latched, arm {latched, pose_source,
   hands_source, ended_op, latch_ms}, latched, halt_epoch, facing_deg, speed_mps, pose_source: "gt.pose", active
   {id, op}}]`.
Then, on the control thread before its next request or tick (<= 20 ms): the active leg motion ends `canceled` with
`reason: "halt", halt_epoch, halt_reason`, and a lease of epoch <= n is revoked (`body.lease{revoked, reason:
halt}`). The mux latch already made that motion's commands ineffective.

**Idempotent.** A halt whose epoch is <= the latched `halt_epoch` is re-acked (`kind: repeat`, same epoch); one whose
epoch is <= the last resume epoch is a late re-send and is ignored (`kind: stale`, `latched` as it is). So the
runtime's 100 ms re-send (robot/health.py) is safe.

**Latch and resume.** While latched, a gated command (§3.11) whose `control_epoch` is missing or <= `halt_epoch` is
rejected `halted`; one with a newer `control_epoch` clears the latch (implicit resume, `body.resumed{source:
implicit:<op>}`). `resume{epoch}` with `epoch >= halt_epoch` clears it (`body.resumed{epoch, was_latched,
halt_epoch, source, arm}`); an older epoch is `stale_command`. On resume the mux leaves an IDLE hold at the latched
facing (never a stale walk) and the arm channel keeps the latched pose until a new owner streams with
`control_epoch > halt_epoch` or an `end`/`release`/`stop {arms: true}` blends it back (§3.9). After the resume a
fenced command with `control_epoch <= halt_epoch` is `stale_command`: every execution of that epoch was ended by the
halt. Commands without fence fields (M1 tools) work again after the resume.

**Client.** `BodyClient.halt(epoch, timeout_s=0.03)` sends on the lane (the PUSH is connected in `connect()`, so a
halt never pays a connect) and waits for `body.halted{epoch}` on its SUB: `{acked, epoch, rtt_ms (send → body.halted
received by the SUB thread), wait_ms, body}`. `BodyClient.resume(epoch)` is the ROUTER op.

### 3.11 Fences and leases (M2b B.2; owner body-core)

`body/fence.py`. PLAN §5.5's fence chain, at the body.

- **Fence fields** on any request (top level or `args`, `args` wins): `execution_id` (str), `generation` (int),
  `control_epoch` (int). Optional: M1 tools send none and behave as in M1. Op records keep them (`status{id}.op.fence`)
  and a motion's `accepted` event echoes them (`data.fence`).
- **Gated ops**: `stand, walk, turn_to, go_to, velocity` (the first message and stream updates), `approach, arm,
  arm_script, scan`. Never gated: `stop, halt, resume, recover, status, ping, hello, bye, release, clear_fault,
  reload_map, shutdown_control`. A refused op gets its op record and a `failed` event with the reason (stream updates
  of `velocity`/`arm` get the reply only).
- **Rules**, in order: (1) the halt latch (§3.10): `halted`; (2) after a resume, `control_epoch <= halt_epoch` →
  `stale_command`; (3) `generation` below the highest generation the body has accepted → `stale_command` (a correction
  bumped the generation and ended older executions); (4) a lease is held and `execution_id != lease.owner` (or no
  `execution_id`) → `body_busy`. Every `stale_command` is also published: `[b"body.stale_command", {id, op, reason,
  why: control_epoch|generation|resume_epoch, execution_id, generation, control_epoch, generation_floor | halt_epoch}]`.
- **Leases.** `acquire{execution_id, generation, control_epoch, mode}` → `lease {lease_id, owner, generation,
  control_epoch, mode, session, t_wall}`; the owner re-acquiring gets the same lease (mode updated). Another owner →
  `body_busy`, unless its generation is newer: then the old lease is revoked (`body.lease{revoked, reason:
  superseded, by}`) and the old owner's leg motion ends `canceled` (reason superseded). `release{execution_id}` ends
  it (`not_owner` otherwise) and cancels the owner's leg motion if it is still running (reason released); the arms
  are left alone (a CarryLock hold survives the release). A halt revokes the leases of its epoch; a lost runtime
  session revokes the lease acquired under it (§3.12). Events: `[b"body.lease", {event: acquired|released|revoked,
  lease, reason?}]`. `mode` (`ANY, LOCOMOTION, ARM_STREAM, ARM_SCRIPT, MANIP`) is recorded, not enforced.
- `body.state`: `lease`, `latched`, `halt_epoch`, and `fences {latched, halt_epoch, resume_epoch, latch_reason,
  latched_for_s, generation_floor, epoch_seen, lease, sessions, stats}`.

Tests: `body/tests/test_lease_fence_unit.py` (rules), `test_lease.py` (over the wire: body_busy, stale generation
dropped and published, supersede, release, stop never gated, a halt revoking the lease, the arm op under a lease).

### 3.12 Modes, faults, soft recovery, runtime sessions (M2b B.3; owner body-core)

**`body.state.mode`**, computed every tick: `ESTOP` (after `shutdown_control`) > `FAULT` (a latched fault) >
`TRANSITION` (`stand` or `recover` running) > `OFF` (not in control) > `LOCOMOTION` (`walk, velocity, turn_to, go_to,
approach` running) > `ARM_STREAM` (`ArmChannel.state().mode` in `stream, chunk, script` and nothing owns the legs) >
`HOLD` (also while latched: `latched` is its own field). `speed` = planar speed and `yaw_rate` from gt.pose
(`pose_source: "gt.pose"`, the body's only pose source). Every change is published: `[b"body.mode", {mode, prev,
prev_s, t_mono, active, arm_mode, fault, latched}]`.

**Faults**, `[b"body.fault", {kind, fault, latched, ...}]`:
- `fell`: `gt.pose.fallen` or pelvis_z < 0.55 m after start. The active op fails `fallen`, planner IDLE, fault
  `fallen` latched (mode FAULT), the arm override dropped, and P1's band engaged at once (`band{on}` from a worker
  thread, never blocking the loop; `--no-fault-band` turns that off). Cleared by `recover` or `clear_fault`
  (`body.fault{cleared: true, by}`).
- `deploy_lost`: `g1_debug` older than `deploy_lost_s` (1.0 s) after the deploy has been in control and while
  control is started (not after `shutdown_control`): the active op fails `deploy_stale`, fault `deploy_lost`, band on.
  Cleared by itself once the deploy has been back in control for 1 s (`by: deploy_back`); `stand` is allowed during
  this fault (a restarted deploy needs it). PLAN §6.6 says 300 ms; 1.0 s = the existing `deploy_stale` watchdog, so
  timing jitter on a loaded box does not engage the band.
- `policy_lost`: an arm chunk session (GR00T) ended by its own watchdog (the policy client stopped sending). An event
  only: the session's `hold_on_end` applies (§3.9) and the mode is not FAULT.

**`recover`** (PLAN §7.3.3 path A, labelled `sim_recovery`; the deploy stays in CONTROL, planner IDLE throughout,
never `command{stop}`): band on → hold `band_hold_s` (2 s) → P1 `reset_robot{x, y, yaw}` (default: where the pelvis
is, current yaw; joints to default, band engaged; P1 logs it) → `stable_s` (2 s) continuously in control, not fallen,
pelvis_z 0.70-0.90 m, |v| < 0.1 m/s (else `recovery_unstable` after 15 s) → band release (ramp 1.5 s) → watch 3 s
(else `recovery_fell`, band on again) → the fault is cleared, `recoveries` += 1. Without a fault it is `no_fault`
(`force: true` runs it anyway, for tests). A dead deploy is `deploy_not_running`: path B (a P2 restart, m1_down/m1_up).

**Runtime sessions** (PLAN §6.6 runtime liveness; opt-in, so M1 tools without pings are unaffected):
`hello{session, watchdog_s (1.0)}` registers one; `ping{session}` or any request carrying `session` refreshes it;
`bye{session}` removes it. A session silent for `watchdog_s` is lost (`[b"body.session", {event: lost, session,
age_s, held}]`, once per loss; `{event: back}` when it pings again). If the body is moving then (a leg motion, or the
arm channel in `stream`/`chunk`/`script`), the body halts itself: an internal halt (§3.10, `reason: runtime_lost`,
`source: watchdog`) at an epoch above every epoch it has seen, so nothing in flight from the dead runtime can move
the robot again; the runtime resumes with `resume{epoch >= halt_epoch}` (the `hello` reply carries `halt_epoch`,
`resume_epoch`, `epoch_seen`, `generation_floor`, so a restarted runtime can set its epoch above them). The lease
acquired under that session (or without a session) is revoked. `BodyClient.hello(session, watchdog_s, heartbeat_s=0.25)`
pings from its own thread and socket.

Tests: `body/tests/test_modes.py` (mode sequence and events, a fall → band → FAULT → `recover` → HOLD on the fakes,
deploy_lost and its clearing, the session watchdog, M1 tools without a session).

### 3.13 `approach`: short strafing reposition (M2b B.6; owner body-core)

`body/approach.py`, for 0.1-0.4 m moves to a stance pose (longer moves are `go_to`'s: `too_far` beyond `max_dist`
0.6 m). No A*: one straight segment on ground truth; when the body has the map loaded the goal needs 0.12 m of raw
clearance (`goal_in_obstacle`) and the segment 0.05 m (`path_blocked`); it never calls P1 for the map itself.

**What SONIC does with short commands** (live, velocity-op pulses from a stand, `tools/approach_test.py --char`,
`outputs/body_wave/approach-char-20260929-071452`, `-071642`): nothing is repeatable open loop. The first 1 cm of
travel comes 0.24-2.0 s after the command (or never); SLOW_WALK 0.2 m/s travels 0-27 cm forward/back in 1-2 s and
hardly steps sideways (< 3 cm in 2 s); 0.3 m/s steps sideways 0-16 cm, 0.4 m/s 7-50 cm; after IDLE the robot keeps
going -5..+36 cm (more at higher speed); pulses of 0.12-0.5 s at 0.2 m/s move < 2 cm. Before a step the pelvis can shift 1-3 cm (once
9.5 cm sideways) with both feet down, and an IDLE then cancels the step.

**Algorithm** (closed loop on gt.pose): turn first with FacingServo if |yaw error| > 15°; then moves: SLOW_WALK
re-aimed at the goal every tick with the facing held at the goal yaw, speed `v` 0.2 forward/back blended to `v_lat`
0.3 sideways; IDLE when `progress + v_along · T_stop >= distance` (v_along over the last 0.2 s) but not before
`min(distance, floor)`, or when the robot passes the goal; settle 0.5 s still; then another move while the error is
above `aim = 0.8 · pos_tol` (up to `max_attempts` 8), a FacingServo correction, or done. Per direction (fwd, back,
left, right) two terms are learnt: `T_stop` = travel after IDLE / v_along at IDLE, from moves that stepped >= 4 cm
(starts at 0.4 s); `floor` rises 2 cm above the progress at which a move stopped when it netted < 1.5 cm (a cancelled
step), and falls 2 cm after an overshoot by more than `pos_tol` (starts at 0). The service keeps them as its running
estimate for the next op (`body.state.approach_est`; in memory, a body restart starts from the defaults).
`succeeded`: pos_err <= aim and |yaw_err| <= yaw_tol after a settle, or pos_err <= pos_tol once the attempts are used
up; `failed(final_error)` otherwise. Result: `pos_err, yaw_err_deg, attempts, turns, moves[] {dir, d0, progress_at_idle,
v_idle, progress, glide, t_first_1cm_s, t_stop_sample, t_stop, floor, err_after}, t_stop, floor, pose_source`.

**Measured** (live, procthor-train-38 living room around (4.0, 6.8), `tools/approach_test.py --n 30`: goals 0.10-0.40
m away in a random body-frame direction, within 0.45 m of the start, half with a yaw change up to ±20°, tol [0.05,
